---
title: Application rules
description: How the brand changes intensity across product UI, marketing, email, and documentation.
---

The brand shows up on many surfaces. The rules shift with context — what counts as "on-brand" in a marketing page is wrong in a transactional email. Each section below has a different "line" between restraint and expression.

## Product UI

The primary brand surface. Most users encounter Nivoda through the product.

- Brand expression is **restrained**. The product should feel professional and functional, not promotional.
- Logo appears in the navigation bar only — never repeated within page content.
- Primary brand colour is used sparingly: CTAs, active states, key accent moments. It is not a background colour.
- Typography follows the design system scale exactly. No bespoke sizing in product UI.
- Copy follows the writing style guide. No marketing language in product UI.
- Illustrations and empty states are the main opportunity for brand personality within the product.

**The line:** Product UI is a tool, not a billboard. Users are here to transact. Brand should build trust and clarity, never compete with the task.

## Marketing

Website, landing pages, campaigns.

- Brand expression is **confident**. This is where the brand's personality comes through most.
- Full visual palette is available — hero imagery, brand colours as backgrounds, display typography.
- Copy register shifts to warm and persuasive.
- Social proof, trust signals, and value propositions are the priority.
- Photography must follow the [photography](/docs/brand/photography) rules.

**The line:** Marketing is a story. It should feel polished, intentional, and human. But it still speaks like Nivoda — never generic, never hyperbolic.

## Email & notifications

- **Transactional emails** (order confirmation, shipping updates) follow product UI rules: clear, concise, action-oriented.
- **Marketing emails** follow marketing rules but at reduced intensity — the inbox is personal space.
- Subject lines: no ALL CAPS, no clickbait.
- Every email must have a single clear CTA.
- Notification copy must be scannable in under 5 seconds.

## Documentation & help

- Voice register: precise, patient, structured. Never casual, never promotional.
- Step-by-step instructions with numbered lists.
- Screenshots must use current UI — never outdated screenshots.
- Technical terms defined on first use.
- Write for the least experienced reader — never assume expertise.
